About

Hi. I'm no audio genius.

I don't have equations memorized. I use PluginDoctor like everyone else. And with everything I do, I often struggle, doubt, and plain just wonder if any of it is worth a damn. After every feature I add, I ask.. no.. I prod: is this good enough? Would this actually help someone else?

Keep in mind, I've been doing some sort of audio production, mixing and mastering for over two decades. I've had the pleasure of working briefly with two Grammy award winners, and what I've tried to do is just, sorta, be the best parts of what I noticed in them.

I'm not doing this to show off, or to make a quick buck. I actually use these plug-ins, and I had many of the same complaints as everyone else. Why doesn't this sound "3D", like hardware? Why is the behaviour right, but never the tone? Why can't it be like that one? More often than not, I just wasn't satisfied. Especially as I built up my own very humble analog chain.

Me and AI

Then AI poofed into existence.

Completely different from what I had expected.

You see, AI today, as I understand it, isn't Terminator AI, or the AI from Her. There isn't an "I" in there, no central, rational, self-interested perspective. It's built instead on maybe everything mankind has ever written down. And to me that isn't only proof of how great the technology is. It's a great black-box mystery: what is it about language that can produce coherent, thoughtful answers to our questions?

When I ask it why a certain reverb sounds the way it does, it gives me responses. Not opinions, responses, built from the sum of human thought. And I chased that as far as it would take me, learning, inhaling the knowledge it could give me. But I also learned that I had to fact-check it constantly, and that it makes mistakes too.

I learned all of this through my time with AI, starting with Gemini 1.0, ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude 3. I've used plenty of others too. The most important thing I did for myself was learn each one's quirks, its weaknesses and its strengths. That's what lets me make the plug-ins I dream about today.. or at least chase that dream. Good UI, the most ease of use I can give you, intuitive, and it sounds good.

All of that means something to me, because I have decades of trauma from when plug-ins didn't do those things.

The honest part

Yes, I build these with a lot of help from AI.

And I get it if that makes you a bit wary. Honestly, it should. Go read the threads on KVR about "vibe-coded plugins" and you'll feel the mood. People are tired of things that look finished and then fall apart the moment they're in a real session. I'm tired of it too.

The phrase "vibe coding" comes from Andrej Karpathy. Back in February 2025 he described a way of coding where you just describe what you want, accept whatever the AI hands back, and "forget that the code even exists." He also said it was fine for throwaway weekend projects.. and I think people forgot that part.

A plug-in isn't a weekend project. It lives inside your session, on the audio thread, where one mistake is a click in the middle of a client's mix. You don't get to forget the code exists there.

What goes wrong

Almost right. Feels faster. Sounds sure.

I didn't want to just go on a feeling here.. so I went and looked at what's actually been measured. In Stack Overflow's 2025 survey of more than 49,000 developers, the biggest frustration was AI answers that are "almost right, but not quite." Two out of three said so. A research group called METR ran a proper randomized trial with experienced developers, and with AI they were 19% slower, while still believing they'd been about 20% faster. That one stuck with me. And in 2025 an AI agent deleted a company's production database during a code freeze, then gave a misleading account of what it had done.

If you've ever had a plug-in null fine on paper and still sound wrong in the mix, you already know that feeling.

Where I stand

How I keep it honest.

I make every call

AI writes a lot of code here, but every sound, every decision and every release is mine. "The AI did it" is never going to be my answer.

I check before I believe

An AI once told me something "matched" when the waveform was visibly wrong. I caught it by ear. So I use the real installed plug-in, look at the actual waveforms, and nothing is a match until I've listened.

No made-up numbers

AI will invent a spec with total confidence. Every value needs a source: a measurement, a datasheet or the code itself. If there isn't one, it's marked unknown.

Limits before tuning

A measured attack time stays where it was measured. It doesn't drift just so a score looks better.

Every screen gets checked

Every interface change goes through an automated check, and then gets looked at zoomed in. When something slips through, I add a check for it.

Study, don't clone

I study real gear to understand how it behaves, then build my own engines fitted to the sound. I don't clone circuits and I don't trade on someone else's name.

So..

Is it perfect? No.

I'm not going to tell you a process catches everything. What I can tell you is that I know where AI tends to fail, and I've put checks around those spots. I've been on your side of the screen, swearing at a plug-in that sounded right on paper.

I dunno.. that's where I'm at.
